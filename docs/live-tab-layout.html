What the measurements said

The before column is candidate A on this page, which carries the shipped rules verbatim, at 1024×768 with the tuning card expanded. The after column is the running app — Electron behind electron-vite, sidecar with in-process YOLO11, models/scanncart-grocery-v1.pt, capture running — measured at 1024×730, the tablet window's content area. B therefore faced a 38px tighter frame than A did, which is the direction that makes the comparison conservative rather than flattering.

The preview row is the app's geometry. This page draws B on a 16:9 preview, so its own readout for that row says 32% of 649×365 where the app measured 33% of 516×387 — the app's camera delivers 4:3, and no amount of CSS changes that.

The item log is the one region whose height the layout does not protect: at the window's content area the rail gives it 139px, which is two fully-visible rows. At a 768-tall content area — the same window without a title bar's worth of chrome — the same layout gives it 177px and four rows. Everything else on the tab is fixed and visible at both.

The console also costs preview area: 33% of the view where the rail layout managed 43%, because the band takes 215px and the camera delivers 4:3, so the preview is height-bound inside a column it could otherwise fill. That is the trade the layout makes, and it is the one number here that argues for A.

The flicker, measured

On an empty counter these weights produce a phantom every few frames, and the shipped conditional tile changed the strip's height by 42px each time — which re-wrapped the auto-fit grid, resized the rail, and dragged the tuning card and the item log with it, two or three times a second. In the console, suppressed and shed moved into a flags line whose height is reserved at a pinned 18px. Injecting one flag and then a second into the running app left every box identical: stats 238, flags 18, log 139, log top 348, preview 387 — in all three states.

The reserve has to match the line box exactly, which is why it is 18px and not 1.5em: 1.5em of 11.5px is 17.25px against an 18.4px line, and the difference was 1px of movement per phantom — small, and exactly the kind of jitter this exists to remove.

Four defects the exercise found

None of these were introduced by B; they were in the shipped layout, and rendering it at a known size is what made them visible.

  1. The item log collapsed to zero height whenever the tuning card was expanded. Both were flex items in one column: the tuning card's basis was its full content, the log's was 0, and the arithmetic handed the whole shortfall to the log. A working capture looked like a capture that had logged nothing.
  2. letterbox drew over the tile beside it. At 20px mono the word is about 108px wide and the strip's tracks were minmax(90px, 1fr). Two tiles overlapping read as the two facts in the same place.
  3. The "no frames yet" placeholder was a grid item, so it landed in the first of four 90px cells and wrapped to three lines — 77px of empty strip where one line was meant.
  4. Tiles inherited line-height: 1.6, spending 32px on a 20px number and 18px on the 11px label under it. In a 320px rail holding four cards' worth of content, that is the difference between an item log and no item log.

What B changed

  • The camera controls left the rail. 13 controls, three of whose labels are sentences ("Drop whole-frame phantoms (three edges)"), cannot be read in a 320px column. As a band they need 215px of height and no scroll.
  • Fields became rows. Label, value and slider on one line is 19px where a stacked field is 45px, which is what makes 13 of them fit under a preview.
  • Tiles became a fixed set, and stable text became rows. Fast-changing numbers (infer/capture fps, latency, tracked) stay tiles in a fixed 2×2; session-long facts (geometry, requirement, recall, classes) became label/value rows, which cannot reflow and have room for their values.
  • The rail became a fixed track and the preview took the remainder, so a wider window widens the feed instead of stretching a column of numbers.

What this page is not

It is not the app. Candidate B here is a drawing at reduced fidelity: it does not carry the live frame, the item log's real rows, the banner stack, or the tooltips. Candidate A is the shipped rules copied out of LiveView.css at the time of writing, and if that file moves on, A here becomes a picture of a layout nobody ships — which is why the numbers above are stamped with a date rather than asserted as current.
